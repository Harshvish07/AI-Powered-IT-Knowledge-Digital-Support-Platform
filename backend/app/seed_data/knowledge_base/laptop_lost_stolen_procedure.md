# Laptop Lost or Stolen Procedure

If your company laptop is lost or stolen, act quickly — the steps below are ordered by
urgency. Every minute matters for limiting exposure of company data.

## Step 1: Report it immediately

Call the IT security hotline (available 24/7) as soon as you realize the device is missing.
Do not wait until normal business hours, and do not wait until you're certain it's truly lost
rather than just misplaced — it's always better to report a false alarm than to delay a real
incident. Have your employee ID and, if known, the approximate time and location the device
was last seen.

## Step 2: Remote actions IT will take

Once notified, IT security will, typically within minutes:

1. Revoke the device's access tokens and active sessions for all company systems.
2. Trigger a remote lock, which displays a message with a contact number on the lock screen if
   the device is later powered on.
3. If the device has been offline for an extended period or the incident involves suspected
   theft, IT security may trigger a remote wipe, which erases all data on the device the next
   time it connects to the internet.

## Step 3: Change your passwords

Even though the laptop itself gets locked out, change your company account password as an
extra precaution, particularly if you believe the device was stolen (rather than simply lost)
and the laptop was unlocked or logged in at the time.

## Step 4: File a police report if stolen

For theft (as opposed to an accidental loss), file a police report. IT security can provide a
letter confirming the device's asset tag and approximate value if needed for the report or for
insurance purposes.

## What NOT to do

Do not attempt to use "find my device" consumer tools yourself to try to track down or
confront whoever has the laptop — leave recovery attempts to IT security and, if applicable,
law enforcement. Your safety matters more than the hardware.

## After the incident

Once the situation is resolved, IT will issue a replacement laptop, restore your standard
software and access from the most recent backup of your profile, and file an asset-loss report
for the company's insurance and inventory records. There is no disciplinary consequence for
reporting a lost or stolen device — the only mistake is not reporting it promptly.
