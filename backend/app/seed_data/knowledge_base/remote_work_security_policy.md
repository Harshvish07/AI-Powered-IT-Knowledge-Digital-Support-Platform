# Remote Work Security Policy

This policy sets minimum security expectations for employees working outside a company office,
whether from home, a co-working space, or while traveling.

## Device requirements

Only company-managed devices may be used to access internal systems or company data. Personal
computers should never be used to access source code, customer data, or internal admin tools,
even temporarily. Company laptops must have full-disk encryption and endpoint protection
enabled at all times — both are configured automatically and should never be disabled.

## Network requirements

Connect the VPN client (see the VPN Setup Guide) before accessing any internal system when
working from a network outside the office. Home Wi-Fi networks should use WPA2 or WPA3
encryption with a non-default router password. Public Wi-Fi (cafes, airports, hotels) should be
treated as untrusted — always use the VPN on these networks, and avoid accessing highly
sensitive systems from them if a more trusted network is available.

## Physical security

Lock your screen whenever you step away from your device, even at home. When working in public
spaces, be mindful of "shoulder surfing" — use a privacy screen filter if you regularly work
with sensitive information in public. Never leave a company laptop unattended in a public place
or visible in an unattended vehicle.

## Video calls and screen sharing

Be aware of what's visible in your background during video calls, and double-check which
window or screen you're sharing before starting a screen share — it's easy to accidentally
expose an unrelated document or chat window. When in doubt, share a specific application window
rather than your entire screen.

## Data handling while remote

Avoid printing company documents on personal printers, and avoid saving company files to
personal cloud storage (personal Google Drive, Dropbox, etc.) or personal email accounts.
Company-approved cloud storage and email are already accessible remotely through the VPN or
directly over the internet where applicable, so there's rarely a legitimate need to route data
through personal accounts.

## Reporting incidents

If you suspect your remote work setup has been compromised in any way — a lost device, a
suspicious network, a phishing attempt that you may have interacted with — report it
immediately through the same channel described in the Laptop Lost/Stolen Procedure. Acting
quickly limits the potential impact far more than waiting to be certain something went wrong.
